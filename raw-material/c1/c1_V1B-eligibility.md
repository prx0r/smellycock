# C1 — V1-B: the prostration, eligibility, Gītā-bhaktas

> The commentary on "having attained, in some way" (kathaṃcit) opens up what the prostration is and who
> is eligible to make it. The prostration is *two-resting*: it must, being conveyed through one of the
> two [the Lord and the devotee], culminate in the rest of both. The "jaya" words, moved by the Lord's
> excess, must have the self-subordinating as their support; the "namaḥ" words, moved by the
> self-subordinating re-recollection, must rest in the Lord's excess. The two are one thing, two-aspect.
>
> The attainment of the Lord is the attainment of servant-ness (dāsya), and the servant-ness is the
> heart of the eligibility. The four Gītā-devotees — the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the
> seeker of wealth, the knower — are all eligible, in their gradations, for the dāsya and the
> recognition. The "kathaṃcit" (in some way) says the dāsya is extremely hard to get; the more is the
> self-subordination stated.

**Terms:** dāsya (servant-ness) · the two-resting prostration · samyagvyutpādaka (the right teacher).

**See also:** V1-A · IPK 1.1 · the Gītā (the four bhaktas).
