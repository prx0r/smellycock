# C1 — V1-A: the spontaneity of the Lord

> The very opening of the IPVV. The maṅgala has been made; now Abhinavagupta expounds the first kārikā —
> the spontaneity (svācchandya) of the Lord. The blue and the other things, without abandoning their
> blue-ness, shine forth and are one with the manifestation (prakāśābhedamayāḥ). This is the Lord's
> spontaneity: being of the nature of consciousness, he shines as the varied knowers and knowns, with a
> body that appears as more than the consciousness-form even though it is not more — and he is aware of
> this (vimṛśati). The image is the mirror: the mirror reflects the wall, the house, the elephant, the
> horse, the person, the pot, the potter — all the distinctions — without being more than its own
> surface. But the mirror does not know that it reflects; its not being aware is its inertia (jaḍatva).
> The param-eśvara, shining thus, *is* aware of himself as shining.
>
> The time-objection — "then it would always shine so" — is answered: the "always" (śaśvat) has no
> independent time against which it could fail; "at this time" means nothing other than the sun's
> movement. There is no time outside the light. Hence the four epithets: śaśvat (un-cut by time),
> acchanna (never other than its own form), svaccha (clear — able to appear as more while being only
> itself), svacchanda (spontaneous — resting in its own freedom). The prostration is the entry
> (samāveśa) into this self-aware Śiva.

**Terms:** svācchandya (spontaneity) · prakāśābhedamaya (one with the manifestation) · vimarśa ·
śaśvat/acchanna/svaccha/svacchanda.
